Annate Bitherapeutics is excited to share that the company has been awarded a **$400K SBIR Phase I grant** from the **National Cancer Institute (NCI)** to advance **ANN-01M**, our lead bispecific antibody program for **relapsed/refractory multiple myeloma (RRMM)**. <!--more-->

ANN-01M is a first-in-class bispecific antibody designed to capture endogenous **ApoL1-complexes** circulating in the blood and deliver them directly into myeloma cells, triggering tumor cell death through a mechanism that is immune-cell-independent.

Because ANN-01M does not rely on a patient's T cells, or any other immune cell population, to work, this approach is designed to sidestep two of the biggest limitations facing today's myeloma immunotherapies: the patient-fitness bar that keeps many patients from qualifying for CAR-T, and the immune cell exhaustion that can blunt bispecific T-cell engagers over time.

This award will support continued preclinical validation of ANN-01M, including in vitro and advanced in vivo efficacy studies and work toward IND-enabling data.

Annate is grateful to the **National Institutes of Health** and the **National Cancer Institute** for this support, and to the scientific and clinical guidance of our consulting team, including **Gregory Lesinski, PhD**, **Ajay Nooka, MD**, **Brian Longstreet**, and **Marty Moore, PhD**. We also thank **Melissa Li, PhD**, for her encouragement and advice throughout this journey.

Track along with us as we work to advance ANN-01M toward the clinic.
